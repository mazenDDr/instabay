# Anchored rating rubric

Rate the final captions against the frozen brand, shared brief and **reference-only** media.
Use all five dimensions independently. Do not infer real resort amenities from reference
photographs. A deterministic rule failure remains a failure even if the rubric mean is high.
Hard rules and human approval are separate from these scores. The canned fixture scores
are illustrative; these anchors are for real raters and future independent judge cassettes.

| Brand dimension | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Voice | Contradicts relaxed brand; aggressive or inappropriate | Mostly generic promotional copy with substantial tone problems | Understandable relaxed tone with noticeable generic or pushy phrases | Consistent restrained tone with one minor awkward phrase | Natural, specific and consistently calm; no unwanted promotion |
| Pillar fit | No connection to planned pillar | Only a vague connection; main idea belongs elsewhere | Planned pillar is identifiable but weakly developed | Clear pillar angle with one small distraction | Hook, message and CTA all serve the planned pillar |
| Caption–visual match | Major unsupported or contradictory scene/facility claims | Several mismatches or misleading property implications | Generally compatible but describes one unsupported detail | Describes observable/reference-approved detail with a small omission | Precisely matches the approved/reference scene without invented facilities |
| Hashtag policy | Violates required count/branded policy or contains misleading tags | Multiple irrelevant or repetitive tags | Counts pass but relevance/tier balance needs editing | Relevant, unique tier mix with one weak choice | Relevant, unique brand/place/niche/broad mix; required limits met |
| Format/CTA | Wrong format or no usable action; reel concept absent when required | CTA and structure poorly suit the planned format | Format works with a generic or weak CTA | Appropriate structure and natural CTA with minor improvement possible | Hook, structure and CTA work together for the specified format |

Brand score is the arithmetic mean of these five values, averaged over three judge samples
and then posts. Human raters label 20 posts; kappa uses the five anchored categories, not
an arbitrary pass/fail conversion. A caption cannot authorize publishing by scoring well.

| Arabic dimension | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fluency | Frequent errors prevent understanding | Many grammar/spelling errors disrupt reading | Understandable with several edits needed | Fluent with a minor grammar or punctuation issue | Fully fluent with sound grammar and clear punctuation |
| Naturalness | Literal calques or broken phrasing dominate | Unusual phrasing throughout | Mostly natural with noticeable translation-like phrases | Natural with one small awkward expression | Reads as independently composed Arabic, idiomatic and effortless |
| Brand voice | Wrong register or tone; contradicts the constitution | Mostly generic or forceful register | Light MSA recognizable but uneven | Consistent light MSA and calm tone with a small lapse | Native calm voice fully aligned with the constitution and intended audience |

For blind Arabic rating, do not reveal version labels or English captions. Rate each opaque
item independently; do not guess its origin. Mean quality is the mean of the three Arabic
dimensions. A native–MT tie contributes one half to native win rate. Interval alpha treats
the mean quality scale as interval data; it requires multiple raters and nonzero variance.

Generic negative controls are scored with the same brand rubric and reference context.
They should lack a specific brand/pillar angle. Their prerecorded lower scores test the
harness, not the independent accuracy of a live judge. Record judge/human agreement before
using real judge scores as evidence of content quality.
